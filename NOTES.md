## SafetyLimiter / flashcheck (M2) — recorded by the safety agent

### Decisions
- S1 Limiter criterion (frontend/src/safety/flashDetector.ts, constants in thresholds.ts). Per signal a hysteresis extremum tracker; a *transition* = change >= 0.10 relative luminance between successive extrema with the darker state < 0.80 (successive transitions always oppose). Signals: 64x36 tile means, two point probes per tile (fine patterns reversing inside a tile), and the whole-frame mean. **Engage** when > 25% of tiles (or of probes) have ENGAGE_AT = 2 opposing transitions, i.e. 3 transitions, inside the 1 s window, OR the whole-frame mean does (any area). Saturated-red tiles (R/(R+G+B) >= 0.8, R > 0.2, linear) use delta 0.05 and engage at the first opposing pair (2 transitions). Stays engaged until a full 1 s passes with no qualifying frame, then releases over 1 s. **Margin:** engages at 1.5 general flashes / 1 red flash per second vs the ceiling of 3; the whole-frame trigger ignores the 25% area condition. A 1 Hz square wave (2 transitions/s) does not engage by design.
- S2 Limiter output while engaged: EMA low-pass of the input (k = 1 - exp(-dt/0.15 s), capped at 0.15) and the output moves from the previous OUTPUT frame toward the EMA with a per-pixel luminance slew limit of 0.2 per second (chroma 3x), applied globally. A 0.10 change therefore takes >= 0.5 s, i.e. at most 1 flash/s can pass the limiter even at its worst; a 10 Hz full-amplitude flicker settles to its temporal mean (mid-grey) with residual < 0.01. The lead's suggested "k <= 0.15 alone" leaves a 0.24 residual at 60 fps (computed), and a per-frame clamp of 0.04 allows 0.12 per half-period at 10 Hz, so the slew is defined per second instead (frame-rate independent). Before engagement the raw input is shown: at most 2 raw transitions (1 flash) for general content, 1 for red.
- S3 Always on (not only when engaged): mean-luminance soft cap (identity below 0.70, smoothly saturating at 0.80, so a full-white frame is shown at 0.80 mean) and a large-area contrast guard: when > 20% of tiles have Michelson contrast > 0.45, local contrast is compressed toward the 5x5-tile neighbourhood mean to <= 0.40 (released over 1 s). This is a backstop for the §4.1 pattern rule (stricter than 0.5 / 25%); without it the very first frames of a high-contrast stripe pattern are a pattern violation. Side effect: large dark scenes with bright fine detail (e.g. starfields) get dimmer highlights while > 20% of tiles are high contrast. Layers should keep contrast in check at the source (§4.1).
- S4 Colour pipeline: the scene target (half-float) holds LINEAR RGB; the limiter measures linear Rec. 709 luminance and writes sRGB-encoded output itself (manual encode in the final blit; ShaderMaterial does no conversion). flashcheck decodes sRGB to linear, so both use the same luminance. Layers must write linear colour into the scene target.
- S5 flashcheck counts flashes = transitions / 2 in every sliding 1 s window (frames at the capture fps), over > 25% of tiles, > 25% of point probes, or the whole frame; red on R*(saturation weight) per tile; patterns via block FFT (8x4 blocks, peak >= 3 cycles/block, >= 30% AC energy, Michelson from 2nd/98th percentiles of linear luminance). A large periodic pattern (contrast >= 0.1, > 25% area) whose probes exceed 2*PATTERN_HZ_MAX transitions per second is a "pattern motion" violation.
- S6 Real-time capture: CDP Page.startScreencast (PNG) in /usr/bin/google-chrome headed on DISPLAY=:1, GPU (ANGLE OpenGL on the RTX 4090, WebGL2 OK: not software rendering). The desktop session on :1 is locked/blanked (org.gnome.ScreenSaver active), so GNOME presents at 1 Hz and rAF stalled at ~1 fps; `--disable-gpu-vsync` makes Chrome use its own ~60 Hz frame source. Achieved: 60 source frames/s, max gap ~20 ms, resampled onto a 30 fps grid. Specs start recording before navigating so the capture includes the first, not-yet-limited frames.
- S7 Safety e2e results (Vite dev server, 5 s at 30 fps, real time): bw10 max 1.0–1.5 general flashes/s; red8 0.5 general / 1.0 red; stripes 1.5–2.0 general (probe/tile), max periodic contrast 0.43, 0 pattern frames; all pass with engagedCount = 1. Frames inspected: bw10 shows one white frame then a smooth glide to mid-grey; red8 holds red and glides darker; stripes appear at contrast ~0.4 and fade to uniform grey.

## Decisions (audio agent, M7)

- D-A1: Buzz locked to the pulse without a free LFO: `PulseTracker` recovers the pulse phase (acos of the value normalised by a peak-hold gain + direction of travel) and frequency (peak-to-peak time), and `AmLock` runs the AM oscillator at the integer multiple n·f_pulse nearest the target rate (4 → 11.5 Hz with max(auditory, body)), kept ≥ 0.3 Hz under the 12 Hz cap, with a software PLL (the oscillator phase is modelled exactly from the setValueAtTime steps, phase error trimmed at 1.2 Hz/cycle). ## Decisions (guard agent: periodicity-aware pattern guard, 2026-10-11)

- **S8 The always-on large-area contrast guard (S3) now counts and compresses only PERIODIC high-contrast area.** What: a tile counts toward the guard fraction only if (the unchanged S3 test) its 5×5-tile neighbourhood has Michelson > `GUARD_TILE_CONTRAST` 0.45 with range > 0.05 **and** its periodicity score p ≥ `GUARD_PERIODICITY_MIN` = 0.5 (new documented constant in `frontend/src/safety/safetyLimiter.ts`; the periodic tiles are 3×3-dilated). The guard engages when that fraction > `GUARD_AREA_FRACTION` 0.2 from the same frame, releases over 1 s, and compresses toward the neighbourhood mean to `GUARD_TARGET_CONTRAST` 0.40 exactly as before, but only on the periodic tiles (3×3-dilated) and on tiles they fully enclose (per-tile weight, 1 s per-tile release so no tile toggles frame to frame). Why: S3 flagged 92–98 % of the tiles of any photo and of dark scenes with bright detail (D-I9, D-I10), so the guard was on almost permanently and flattened the picture into grey blotches — the systemic cause of the "drab" verdict. BUILD.md §4.1 caps contrast only for periodic patterns (stripes, rings, lattices, checkerboards) over > 25 % of the screen, which is what WCAG 2.3 / Ofcom / ITU-R BT.1702 regulate; aperiodic high contrast (a photo, filaments on black, scattered blooms) is not a pattern hazard (flashes stay covered by S1/S2, which are untouched). §0.7: `thresholds.ts`, `tools/flashcheck.py` and every protected test are untouched and no threshold value changes (0.45 / 0.2 / 0.40 / 0.5 / 25 % all stand); the change restricts which content the backstop treats as a "pattern", matching the rule it backs up, and adds a periodicity test that is itself conservative (it catches every periodic pattern flashcheck flags in the tests below, plus thin-line lattices flashcheck does not flag). The protected bw10/red8/stripes/bw10-debug/static cases still pass with the guard on from frame 1 on the stripes.
  * **Algorithm** (`frontend/src/safety/periodicity.ts` is the pure-TS reference; the GPU pass in `limiterShaders.ts` is a literal, generated port). Per tile, profiles through the tile centre along 8 directions (every 22.5°, so a pattern is ≤ 11.25° off a profile and its apparent period ≤ 2 % longer) at four scales, in tile units (resolution independent): coarse 128 samples at 1/7 tile with 2-tap box samples (window 18.3 tiles; periods 0.43–4.6 tiles = up to W/14, 91 px at 1280 wide, 137 px at 1920), fine 128 samples at 1/60 tile (periods 1/30–0.5 tile: 1-px-scale gratings), and the grid of exact-area tile means (step 1 tile; 32 samples along the 8 directions with bilinear interpolation — curved rings up to 8 tiles — and one 48-sample horizontal profile — straight stripes up to 12 tiles = W/5.3, i.e. anything with > 5 cycles across the screen). Score per profile = classic autocorrelation pitch detection with the McLeod–Wyvill NSDF: remove the linear trend (gradients → 0; residual rms < `PERIODICITY_MIN_RMS` 0.004 → 0, which also rejects the perfectly periodic 8-bit quantisation staircase of smooth gradients); r(k) = 2Σd_i d_{i+k} / Σ(d_i² + d_{i+k}²); after r first drops below `PERIODICITY_DROP` 0.3, each local maximum k ≤ N/4 (≥ 4 cycles in the window) that rises above 0.3 again scores min(r(k), max r(2k−1..2k+1)) — the pattern must repeat a second time, which rejects two bright filaments whose single spacing correlates perfectly; p = the best (search stops at 0.98), and the tile's p is the max over its 25 profiles.
  * **GPU implementation:** a luminance pyramid pass (512×288 box-filtered luminance with mips; level 3 = exact-area 64×36 tile means), one fragment per (tile, direction, scale) on a 512×144 target (scale-major so warps rarely diverge), a 64×36 reduce pass writing (minY, maxY, p, pGrid) — the second of the two existing readbacks, so the protected unit tests' mock renderer (which alternates tile/stats readbacks) is unaffected. The score is fully unrolled (generated GLSL, ~200 kB, 7.9k products) so the 128-sample profile stays in registers: the loop version was latency-bound on local memory (1.3–2.3 ms); unrolled it adds ~0.3–0.4 ms. Cold shader compile ~2 s on the first page load (Chrome caches it; later loads ~0.5 s for all limiter shaders).
  * **Also fixed (compression accuracy, pre-existing):** the neighbourhood mean the guard compresses toward was the mean of 64 jittered point samples, which aliases with fine patterns; neighbouring tiles then compressed toward different means and a static 45° 12-px stripe pattern kept a block contrast of 0.54 after compression (flashcheck: 147 pattern frames). The mean now comes from the exact-area tile means (pyramid level 3): 0.425, 0 pattern frames.
  * **Measured behaviour** (RESULTS "Guard periodicity"): every periodic test pattern at Michelson 0.9 (rings 30 px, hex 24 px, 45° stripes 12 px, checker 16 px, stripes 80 px, 2-px lines every 40 px, a 36-spoke sunburst, rings 90/120 px, 2-px stripes, 4-px checker, 30°/67.5° gratings, drifting stripes, dark 0.01/0.06 stripes, 30% and 26% area patches) engages the guard from frame 1 and leaves flashcheck with 0 pattern frames.   * **Limits (honest):** curved patterns with periods > ~8 tiles (rings of 160 px at 1280 wide: 94 % of tiles) and straight stripes > 12 tiles (fewer than ~5.3 cycles across the screen) are not seen as periodic; hole filling only covers regions fully enclosed by detected tiles. A sin()-hash "noise" (`fract(sin(dot(p, …)) * 43758.5)`) is lattice-periodic and IS flagged (safe direction). Near the screen edges a profile window is shifted inward, so one profile can decide a block of edge tiles (it amplifies both detections and false positives there; the blobs' 4.8 % is mostly one such block).
