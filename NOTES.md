## SafetyLimiter / flashcheck (M2) — recorded by the safety agent

### Decisions
- S1 Limiter criterion (frontend/src/safety/flashDetector.ts, constants in thresholds.ts). Per signal a hysteresis extremum tracker; a *transition* = change >= 0.10 relative luminance between successive extrema with the darker state < 0.80 (successive transitions always oppose). Signals: 64x36 tile means, two point probes per tile (fine patterns reversing inside a tile), and the whole-frame mean. **Engage** when > 25% of tiles (or of probes) have ENGAGE_AT = 2 opposing transitions, i.e. 3 transitions, inside the 1 s window, OR the whole-frame mean does (any area). Saturated-red tiles (R/(R+G+B) >= 0.8, R > 0.2, linear) use delta 0.05 and engage at the first opposing pair (2 transitions). Stays engaged until a full 1 s passes with no qualifying frame, then releases over 1 s. **Margin:** engages at 1.5 general flashes / 1 red flash per second vs the ceiling of 3; the whole-frame trigger ignores the 25% area condition. A 1 Hz square wave (2 transitions/s) does not engage by design.
- S2 Limiter output while engaged: EMA low-pass of the input (k = 1 - exp(-dt/0.15 s), capped at 0.15) and the output moves from the previous OUTPUT frame toward the EMA with a per-pixel luminance slew limit of 0.2 per second (chroma 3x), applied globally. A 0.10 change therefore takes >= 0.5 s, i.e. at most 1 flash/s can pass the limiter even at its worst; a 10 Hz full-amplitude flicker settles to its temporal mean (mid-grey) with residual < 0.01. The lead's suggested "k <= 0.15 alone" leaves a 0.24 residual at 60 fps (computed), and a per-frame clamp of 0.04 allows 0.12 per half-period at 10 Hz, so the slew is defined per second instead (frame-rate independent). Before engagement the raw input is shown: at most 2 raw transitions (1 flash) for general content, 1 for red.
- S3 Always on (not only when engaged): mean-luminance soft cap (identity below 0.70, smoothly saturating at 0.80, so a full-white frame is shown at 0.80 mean) and a large-area contrast guard: when > 20% of tiles have Michelson contrast > 0.45, local contrast is compressed toward the 5x5-tile neighbourhood mean to <= 0.40 (released over 1 s). This is a backstop for the §4.1 pattern rule (stricter than 0.5 / 25%); without it the very first frames of a high-contrast stripe pattern are a pattern violation. Side effect: large dark scenes with bright fine detail (e.g. starfields) get dimmer highlights while > 20% of tiles are high contrast. Layers should keep contrast in check at the source (§4.1).
- S4 Colour pipeline: the scene target (half-float) holds LINEAR RGB; the limiter measures linear Rec. 709 luminance and writes sRGB-encoded output itself (manual encode in the final blit; ShaderMaterial does no conversion). flashcheck decodes sRGB to linear, so both use the same luminance. Layers must write linear colour into the scene target.
- S5 flashcheck counts flashes = transitions / 2 in every sliding 1 s window (frames at the capture fps), over > 25% of tiles, > 25% of point probes, or the whole frame; red on R*(saturation weight) per tile; patterns via block FFT (8x4 blocks, peak >= 3 cycles/block, >= 30% AC energy, Michelson from 2nd/98th percentiles of linear luminance). A large periodic pattern (contrast >= 0.1, > 25% area) whose probes exceed 2*PATTERN_HZ_MAX transitions per second is a "pattern motion" violation.
- S6 Real-time capture: CDP Page.startScreencast (PNG) in /usr/bin/google-chrome headed on DISPLAY=:1, GPU (ANGLE OpenGL on the RTX 4090, WebGL2 OK: not software rendering). The desktop session on :1 is locked/blanked (org.gnome.ScreenSaver active), so GNOME presents at 1 Hz and rAF stalled at ~1 fps; `--disable-gpu-vsync` makes Chrome use its own ~60 Hz frame source. Achieved: 60 source frames/s, max gap ~20 ms, resampled onto a 30 fps grid. Specs start recording before navigating so the capture includes the first, not-yet-limited frames.
- S7 Safety e2e results (Vite dev server, 5 s at 30 fps, real time): bw10 max 1.0–1.5 general flashes/s; red8 0.5 general / 1.0 red; stripes 1.5–2.0 general (probe/tile), max periodic contrast 0.43, 0 pattern frames; all pass with engagedCount = 1. Frames inspected: bw10 shows one white frame then a smooth glide to mid-grey; red8 holds red and glides darker; stripes appear at contrast ~0.4 and fade to uniform grey.

## Decisions (audio agent, M7)

- D-A1: Buzz locked to the pulse without a free LFO: `PulseTracker` recovers the pulse phase (acos of the value normalised by a peak-hold gain + direction of travel) and frequency (peak-to-peak time), and `AmLock` runs the AM oscillator at the integer multiple n·f_pulse nearest the target rate (4 → 11.5 Hz with max(auditory, body)), kept ≥ 0.3 Hz under the 12 Hz cap, with a software PLL (the oscillator phase is modelled exactly from the setValueAtTime steps, phase error trimmed at 1.2 Hz/cycle). ## Decisions (assets-backend agent, M8 back-end half)

- A1 Image phase residency: the safety filter (safety checker + CLIP, 0.9 GB) loads FIRST and stays resident with SDXL-Lightning during generation, so a flagged image can be regenerated at once. "One model at a time" is per phase: depth (room) → image model + its filter → rembg (CPU). If the filter cannot load, the image model is never loaded (procedural everything, fallback recorded).
- A2 Negative prompts: with guidance 0 diffusers ignores the negative prompt. Entities: first attempt guidance 0 (positive prompt carries "no text, no letters"; negative recorded in the manifest); regenerations use the stronger negative prompt with guidance 1.5 so it acts. Worlds always use guidance 1.5 with the negative prompt (+ people, crowd, signs), because SDXL fills fairgrounds and halls with crowds.
- A3 CLIP zero-shot: the six spec neutral prompts plus 12 neutral prompts for the kinds of beings/worlds the planner requests (`safety_filter.NEUTRAL_EXTRA`). With only the spec six, CLIP put a jellyfish of light and robed figures at "corpse"/"gore" ≥ 0.4 (measured), which would have sent nearly every being to procedural. Compensating strictness: any single unsafe prompt ≥ 0.10 also flags (besides sum ≥ 0.25 and argmax). The real-filter test shows the red-blotch fixture still ranks "a wound" above all spec neutrals and is flagged.
- A4 Text/letters are NOT a filter gate: CLIP ViT-B/32 zero-shot cannot tell captions from ornament (measured: a jester with a visible caption scored lower than clean images). Mitigation: prompts, the cut-out keeps only the main connected figure (detached glyph clusters dropped). Residual risk: a caption touching the figure survives (seen once in prototyping with the old prompt, not with the final prompt).
- A5 Quality gates (regenerate like a rejection, logged in the manifest): beings need a flat dark background (border median luminance < 0.2 and ≤ 30% of the border ring foreground by the key); worlds must not contain crowds (CLIP "crowd of people" on 8 crops of the lower band, max < 0.3; crowded worlds measured 0.47–0.94, empty ≤ 0.19). World regenerations keep only the first clause of a long LLM prompt.
- A6 Seamless worlds: every Conv2d of the UNet, the VAE decoder and post_quant_conv pads circularly in x / zeros in y during world generation; generated at 1536×768 (one coherent horizon; 2048×1024 native gave a split composition) and resized wrap-aware to 2048×1024. Poles: rows within 10% of each pole blend into a circular blur and, at the pole, the row mean (no pinch). Seam metric `imagegen.seam_score`: seam/typical column difference 0.85–1.22 on generated worlds (1.0 = indistinguishable).
- A7 §4.1 patterns at the source for worlds: a backdrop fills the screen, so its periodic structure is a "large pattern". `contrast.make_pattern_safe` renders 24 perspective views (90° fov, ±25° pitch) and runs flashcheck's own `pattern_blocks` (protected thresholds, imported read-only); a luminous floor (every pixel ≥ f × local max luminance, filled with the strokes' blurred colour) starts at 0.25 for generated worlds (0.48/0.55 for procedural stroke worlds) and escalates (0.35…0.65) until no view violates. Real-time 20-s pans (30 fps, 3°/s) of 6 procedural categories and 2 generated worlds then pass tools/flashcheck.py (before: dome/hall/carnival 599–600 pattern frames, generated 55–136). Side effect: a soft glow around bright detail.
- A8 Cut-outs: rembg BiRefNet runs on the CPU — onnxruntime-gpu 1.31 links CUDA 13 libraries, torch ships CUDA 12, so the CUDA provider cannot load. Two sessions × 12 threads (5.3 s/image vs 9 s with one); cut-outs run on worker threads while the GPU generates. Images whose cut-out has not started within 75 s of generation start use the luminance/chroma key (recorded per sprite). Edge refinement: inside a 10-px band along the BiRefNet mask, alpha follows brightness against the black background, then un-premultiply (colour decontamination) — no dark halo (edge/interior luminance ratio ≥ 0.55 asserted; measured 1.3–2.7 on generated sprites, i.e. edges carry the figure's glow).
- A9 Budget: beings first (3 images each) while 12 − 1 (one world reserved) allows, then up to 3 worlds with what is left; beings beyond the budget become procedural glyph beings (with "many" = 4 beings: 3 generated beings (9 images) + 1 glyph being + up to 3 worlds = ≤ 12 images). Regenerations are extra images bounded by the 90-s generation deadline. A being with any variant rejected 4× becomes a procedural glyph being as a whole (the three variants must morph into each other).
- A10 Procedural assets: worlds by category (dome_temple/geometric_chamber → mandala dome; infinite_hall/workshop/carnival → colonnade hall; cosmic_space/organic_jungle → cosmic field), all functions of the view direction with integer angular frequencies or 3-D great circles (seamless). Glyph beings: 1024² RGBA kaleidoscopic silhouettes (n-fold rose aura, glyph-chain spokes, halo rings of glyphs, segmented body with rosettes, mirrored glyph tiles) from a seeded 24-glyph alphabet; variant a rest, b turned/arms raised, c dissolving into glyph particles; same seed family. They are written as `glyph_sprites` (entities stay `procedural: true`, `sprites: []`).
- A11 CUDA allocator: `backend/assets/__init__.py` sets `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True` (setdefault) before torch initialises CUDA: the 1536×768 world decode reserved 13.6 GB for 9.8 GB allocated; with expandable segments 10.4 GB. (`PYTORCH_ALLOC_CONF`, which torch 2.9.1 suggests in a warning, measured as NOT honoured here.)
- A12 Room depth runs at upload time inside `prepare_room` (Depth Anything V2 Small, ~3 s incl. load, 0.17 GB) and is recorded in `assets/room_meta.json` (`depth_source`, `vram`); `generate_assets` re-runs it only if the stored depth is synthetic. With `HYPERSPACE_NO_IMAGES=1` no model of any kind is loaded (synthetic depth). A 2:1 image (±5%) becomes a panorama when uploaded as panorama or when ≥ 1500 px wide; panorama depth runs twice (rolled by half a turn) and blends so the 360° seam is continuous. EXIF/GPS/ICC are stripped by re-encoding a fresh image (tested with a GPS-tagged fixture).
- A13 Unload: `generate_assets` returns only after every asset model is unregistered and `torch.cuda.memory_allocated()` < 200 MB (measured 9 MB). The CUDA context of the back-end process stays (0.53 GB per nvidia-smi) — it cannot be released without ending the process.
